Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Missouri City Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Missouri City, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
